Rockstar and the press who have seen GTA 6 have revealed, piece by piece, how it plays. Here's everything in one place, organized by system: the police, money and theft, playing as Jason and Lucia, and daily life in Leonida. We always separate what Rockstar has said from what reporters interpret.

## The short version

- **Police:** six wanted stars that change color by phase, plus a new **Heat** system that shows what the police know about you — your face, clothes, car, weapon, accomplice or a camera recording.
- **Money** matters more than in any previous GTA: at some points in the story you'll need to raise cash to move forward.
- **Theft:** you can steal "pretty much anything," and expensive cars need tools and an in-game app, **WAiNK**.
- **Jason and Lucia:** you can switch between them any time — even mid-drive. Their relationship changes with how you play, but it's optional.
- **Criminal profile:** it doesn't reward or punish a playstyle, but repeated behavior changes how the world reacts to you.

## Police: six stars and Heat

The wanted level returns with **six stars**, and for the first time the stars change appearance depending on the stage of the chase. This is GamesRadar's reading of what was shown in the extended gameplay look (their interpretation, not an official manual):

| How the stars look | What it means |
|---|---|
| Black and white | Police are investigating a crime but don't know who did it |
| Gray | They have information on you and are actively searching |
| White | They're chasing you and have eyes on you |
| Red | You escaped, but they still hold information on you |

**Heat** appears as red icons in the corner of the screen showing which leads the police have. Six have been seen: **security cameras, accomplice, clothing, face, weapon and vehicle**. The more are active, the harder it is to escape. Each one clears with a specific action — holstering your weapon, changing clothes, ditching the car that gives you away. Covering your face and avoiding cameras helps keep them from triggering.

**Splitting up.** If the police are looking for a man and a woman, going separate ways divides their attention, according to Game Informer. The catch: **if either of them is arrested or killed, it's game over**.

**Changing your look.** Your **personal vehicles have a usable trunk** where you can keep clothes and weapons to change on the street.

## Money and theft

Rob Nelson of Rockstar North has said money will be **more important than in any previous GTA** — not just for buying things, but because at certain points Jason and Lucia need to raise money to move the story forward.

In return, there are more ways to get it. You'll be able to steal **"pretty much anything,"** from headphones in a store to jobs that aren't part of the story, and some targets are found just by exploring.

**Car theft has difficulty levels**, according to Game Informer:

| Type of car | How you steal it |
|---|---|
| Older cars | The old-fashioned way — but they're not the best getaway cars |
| Cars with pickable locks | A crowbar and a minigame, without setting off the alarm |
| Fast, expensive cars | Special tools; luxury models have alarms or trackers you disable with the WAiNK app |
| Others | Key cloners and gadgets you unlock as you progress |

**WAiNK** scans a car's security and also shows what it costs to register as yours and what it's worth if you sell it.

**Weapons.** The classic weapon wheel is back, but with a limit on how many weapons you can carry, closer to Red Dead Redemption 2, and pedestrians react if you have one out, according to Dexerto. Rockstar spent a long time testing alternatives to the wheel before going back to it, as Bloomberg's Jason Schreier has explained.

## Playing as Jason and Lucia

**Switching.** You can switch between the two, and the same scene can be seen from each one's point of view. If they're in the same car, you can switch to the passenger and let the other drive to your destination — fast, and with no penalty, according to Nelson.

**The relationship is optional.** It changes with the time they spend together and how they treat each other: answering calls and texts, going out, holding hands, small gestures like opening the door. It can end up as a strong couple or a simple partnership. Rockstar says it **gives no gameplay advantage and doesn't change the difficulty**; what changes are some lines and situations.

**The criminal profile** is a reputation system, different from Red Dead Redemption 2's honor. What missions ask of you doesn't count either way. What moves it is **unnecessary behavior you repeat**: if you always cause chaos, the world eventually reacts to that, and it can affect how the story unfolds.

## Daily life in Leonida

- **Homes** are closer to RDR 2's camps than to houses in other GTA games, Nelson says. Inside you can do small things like shave or trim your hair; for a new haircut, you go to the barber.
- **Social media:** there's an Instagram parody, Snapmatic and news apps — but Jason and Lucia **won't post anything**. They're wanted criminals.
- **RydeMe** is an Uber-style app for fast travel, where you can chat with other passengers or scroll social media.

## What we still don't know

- How much money you'll need at each point in the story.
- What the criminal profile actually changes.
- How long the couple can stay split up.
- How many cars need WAiNK.
